Word of the day for May 7, 2027 (Day 218)

Collatz conjecture

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The unsolved conjecture that, starting from any positive whole number, if you halve it when it’s even and triple it and add one when it’s odd, you will always end up at 1.

The story

The German mathematician Lothar Collatz is said to have posed it in 1937. Start from 7, for example, and you go 22, 11, 34, 17, 52 and so on, up and down, until you eventually reach 1. A child can follow the rules, yet it remains unproven. The Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdős reportedly said that mathematics may not be ready for such problems. A Japanese company made headlines by offering a prize of over 100 million yen for a proof.

The lens

Even after computers have checked it up to astronomically large numbers, that still isn’t a proof. Few problems show so clearly how the simplest-looking questions can run the deepest.

Drop it casually

“Up and down, up and down, but it always comes back to 1. My boss’s mood is basically the Collatz conjecture.”
