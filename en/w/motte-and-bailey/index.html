Word of the day for February 27, 2027 (Day 149)

Motte-and-bailey

Philosophy

Category: Philosophy哲 学思 考

In short

Making a bold claim, retreating to a modest, easy-to-defend one when challenged, then returning to the bold one once the danger has passed.

The story

The philosopher Nicholas Shackel named it in a 2005 paper. A motte is the stout tower built on a mound in a medieval castle; the bailey is the broad, livable land around it. You live in the roomy bailey (the sweeping claim) day to day, and when attacked you hole up in the motte (the obvious claim that’s hard to argue with).

The lens

Someone who said “all of X is bad” answers pushback with “I only said some of it has problems.” Just checking which claim is on the table keeps the argument from spinning its wheels.

Drop it casually

“You said “always” a minute ago, right? What you’re defending now is the motte. That first claim was the bailey.”
