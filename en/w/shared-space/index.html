Shared space

Architecture & Cities

Category: Architecture & Cities建 築都 市

In short

A road design approach that removes traffic lights, signs, and the curb between sidewalk and street so that pedestrians, cyclists, and drivers share one space and yield to each other.

The story

The Dutch traffic engineer Hans Monderman popularized it. In the town of Drachten in the northern Netherlands, he removed the traffic lights and signs from an intersection carrying more than 20,000 vehicles a day. Drivers began watching their surroundings and driving more carefully, and accidents were reported to have actually fallen.

The lens

Monderman reportedly said that if you treat people like idiots, they’ll behave like idiots. Critics point out, though, that for people with visual impairments, the lack of curbs and signals can make streets more dangerous. The question of safe for whom is still being asked.

Drop it casually

“The more rules we add, the less anyone thinks. Maybe our team needs a little shared space.”
