Seven Bridges of Königsberg

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The puzzle of whether you can walk through a city crossing each of its seven bridges exactly once. The mathematician Euler showed it can’t be done.

The story

In the Prussian city of Königsberg, an island in the river and its banks were linked by seven bridges, and people reportedly wondered whether there was a route that crossed every bridge exactly once. In 1736 the Swiss-born mathematician Leonhard Euler replaced the land with points and the bridges with lines, and showed that because too many land areas had an odd number of bridges, no such route exists.

The lens

Euler’s move of throwing away distance and shape and looking only at what connects to what became the starting point of graph theory. That way of seeing lives on in subway maps, social networks, and the route finder in your car.

Drop it casually

“There’s no way to hit every shop once and get back home. Like the bridges of Königsberg, the streets just don’t connect that way.”
