Word of the day for December 24, 2026 (Day 84)

Money illusion

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

Feeling richer or poorer because of the number on your paycheck, without accounting for how prices have changed.

The story

The economist Irving Fisher popularized the idea with his 1928 book The Money Illusion. If prices rise 5% in a year and your pay rises 3%, you’re actually worse off, yet most people feel they got a raise. Flip it around, and a small pay cut while prices are falling feels outrageous even when nothing has changed in real terms.

The lens

Once you know it, every headline about wage growth makes you look for the inflation number next to it. It’s the gut-level version of economics’ most basic distinction, nominal versus real.

Drop it casually

“I was thrilled about my 3% raise. Then I noticed prices were up 5%. Total money illusion.”
