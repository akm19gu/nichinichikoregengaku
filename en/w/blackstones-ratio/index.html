Blackstone’s ratio

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

The principle of criminal justice that it is better for ten guilty people to go free than for one innocent person to suffer.

The story

The 18th-century English jurist William Blackstone set it down in his Commentaries on the Laws of England (1765–69). Similar ideas are much older, and over the centuries different writers have put the number at a hundred, a thousand, and more.

The lens

It puts a number on the value judgment behind the presumption of innocence. It forces the basic question of any system: how much of each kind of error, convicting the innocent or letting the guilty go, are we willing to accept?

Drop it casually

“If the evidence isn’t clear, don’t punish. By Blackstone’s ratio, that’s the only principled call.”
