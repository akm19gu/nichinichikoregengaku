Hawthorne effect

Society

Category: Society社 会

In short

People behave differently when they know they’re being watched.

The story

The name comes from a series of experiments in the 1920s and 1930s at Western Electric’s Hawthorne Works plant in the US. Productivity rose whether the lighting was made brighter or dimmer, which was interpreted to mean that what affected workers wasn’t the conditions themselves but the attention they were getting.

The lens

Reanalysis of the original data, however, suggests the effect is not that clear, and the term may have taken on a life of its own. Still, it gives you a reason to ask, whenever you read a study, how much being observed shaped the results.

Drop it casually

“Everyone works suspiciously fast on the days the boss walks the floor. The Hawthorne effect.”
