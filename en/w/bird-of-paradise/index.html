Word of the day for September 22, 2027 (Day 356)

Bird-of-paradise

Living Things

Category: Living Things生 き 物

In short

Birds with dazzling ornamental plumes found in New Guinea and nearby. Europeans once believed they were legless birds of heaven.

The story

The first specimens to reach Europe in the 16th century had been prepared locally with the legs and wings removed. So Europeans concluded the birds had no feet, never landed, flew their whole lives, and lived on heavenly dew. In the 18th century Linnaeus, the father of biological classification, gave one species a scientific name meaning “footless.”

The lens

Real male birds-of-paradise spread their plumes and even hang upside down in elaborate courtship dances for females. Their splendor is the product of a long evolutionary history of being chosen by females.

Drop it casually

“If you judge from the specimen alone, you’ll end up like the Europeans who thought birds-of-paradise had no feet.”
