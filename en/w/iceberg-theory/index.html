Iceberg theory

Literature

Category: Literature文 学

In short

A way of writing that deliberately leaves most of the story beneath the surface, so readers feel the weight of the whole from the small part that’s shown.

The story

The American writer Ernest Hemingway described the idea in his 1932 book Death in the Afternoon. If a writer knows something well, he can leave it out and the reader will feel it as strongly as if it were stated. “The dignity of movement of an ice-berg,” he wrote, “is due to only one-eighth of it being above water.”

The lens

The six-word story “For sale: baby shoes, never worn” is often cited as the ultimate example of the theory and attributed to Hemingway, but there’s no solid evidence he wrote it.

Drop it casually

“My boss’s email is one line long and somehow terrifying. A master of the iceberg theory.”
