Bayesian search theory

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

A way of searching for something lost by updating your estimate of where it’s likely to be after each search, then deciding where to look next.

The story

Its key feature is treating “we searched here and found nothing” as a new clue in itself. It was used with success in the 1966 search for a US hydrogen bomb lost off the coast of Spain and the 1968 search for the submarine Scorpion, lost in the Atlantic. The wreckage of Air France Flight 447, which crashed into the Atlantic in 2009, was found in 2011 after this method narrowed the search area.

The lens

A place you searched without finding anything isn’t wasted effort. You’ve learned that the thing probably isn’t there. Turning failure into information is useful well beyond lost objects.

Drop it casually

“No keys. I checked the desk. By Bayesian search, the next best bet is my coat pocket.”
