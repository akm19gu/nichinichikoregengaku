Müller-Lyer illusion

Psychology

Category: Psychology心 理

In short

Two lines of equal length look longer with outward-pointing arrowheads at the ends and shorter with inward-pointing ones.

The story

The German sociologist and psychiatrist Franz Carl Müller-Lyer published it in 1889. Cross-cultural studies in the 1960s reported that people surrounded by right-angled buildings and rooms were more susceptible to the illusion than people living in other environments.

The lens

How we see may depend not only on the eye’s mechanics but on where we grew up. It shows that the world we casually look at is assembled from experience.

Drop it casually

“The lines are the same length, but one still looks longer. The Müller-Lyer illusion fools me every time.”
