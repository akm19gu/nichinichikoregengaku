Cantillon effect

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

The idea that newly created money doesn’t spread evenly through an economy, and those who receive it first benefit most.

The story

It’s named after Richard Cantillon, an Irish-born banker and economist who worked in France in the early 18th century. Those who get new money first can spend it before prices rise. By the time the money reaches people further away, prices have already gone up, and the last to receive it lose out.

The lens

It often comes up in debates about loose monetary policy, where stocks and real estate rise first and wages catch up last. It teaches you to look not only at how much money there is but at where it flows in.

Drop it casually

“My pay only went up after prices did. In Cantillon terms, we’re at the end of the line.”
