Word of the day for March 10, 2027 (Day 160)

Roman à clef

Literature

Category: Literature文 学

In short

A novel that portrays real people and events under changed names.

The story

French for “novel with a key.” Readers who know the key, who each character really is, can see the hidden meaning. The form was fashionable in 17th-century French high society, and later the term came to cover books drawn from the author’s own life, such as Sylvia Plath’s The Bell Jar.

The lens

Hunting for the real people behind a novel’s characters is one of reading’s slightly gossipy pleasures. It changes how you read autobiographical fiction and insider novels about an industry, too.

Drop it casually

“The boss in this novel is obviously modeled on our department head. At the office it’s being read as a roman à clef.”
