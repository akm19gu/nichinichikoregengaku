Kepler conjecture

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The conjecture that the densest way to pack identical spheres is the way grocers stack oranges.

The story

In 1611 the astronomer Johannes Kepler stated it in a little book about snowflakes. One prompt was the scholar Thomas Harriot, assistant to the English explorer Walter Raleigh, who had been thinking about stacks of cannonballs on ships. This seemingly obvious claim wasn’t proved until 1998, when the American mathematician Thomas Hales announced a proof that relied on massive computer calculations.

The lens

Even the experts reviewing the paper could only say they were 99% sure it was right, because the calculations were so vast. So Hales launched a project to have computers check every line of the proof, and finished it in 2014. It makes you think about how hard it is to confirm that something is correct.

Drop it casually

“Whenever I see the pyramid of oranges at the grocery store, I want to tell someone that’s the answer to the Kepler conjecture.”
