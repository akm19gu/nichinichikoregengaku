Cherenkov radiation

Science

Category: Science科 学

In short

The blue glow given off when a charged particle races through a material like water faster than light travels in that material.

The story

Light is the fastest thing in a vacuum, but it slows down a little in water and other materials. A particle moving faster than that creates a shock wave of light, like the sonic boom of a plane breaking the sound barrier. The Soviet physicist Pavel Cherenkov discovered it in 1934 and later shared the Nobel Prize in Physics.

The lens

The eerie blue glow in a nuclear reactor’s pool comes from this light. Super-Kamiokande, a detector deep underground in Gifu, Japan, catches it to observe neutrinos arriving from space and elsewhere.

Drop it casually

“The new hire is moving faster than everyone around them. I swear they’re glowing blue. Cherenkov radiation?”
