Word of the day for April 10, 2028 (Day 557)

Nemawashi

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

Talking things through with the people involved and securing their agreement before a formal decision is made. Originally a gardening term.

The story

When transplanting a large tree, gardeners dig around its roots about a year ahead, cut the thick roots, and let fine new roots grow. That way the tree is less likely to die after the move. This work is called nemawashi, “going around the roots,” and from it the word came to mean the groundwork done in advance to make things go smoothly.

The lens

It’s sometimes introduced in English as nemawashi, as a hallmark of Japanese organizations. Like the tree whose roots are cut, it also shows consideration for people unsettled by sudden change. On the other hand, it’s criticized for letting the real debate happen outside the meeting.

Drop it casually

“The more important the proposal, the more of it gets decided in the nemawashi the day before. The meeting itself is just transplanting day.”
