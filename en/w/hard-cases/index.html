Word of the day for February 13, 2027 (Day 135)

Hard cases make bad law

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

A legal maxim: building rulings or laws around unusual cases that stir strong sympathy produces rules that work badly for ordinary cases.

The story

It’s known as a phrase used in 19th-century English court rulings. In a 1904 dissent, US Supreme Court Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes wrote that “great cases, like hard cases, make bad law,” warning that the more public attention a case draws, the more momentary feelings distort sober judgment.

The lens

When tough regulations or new laws are rushed through right after a heartbreaking incident, this maxim comes to mind. Then again, it can also become an excuse for the coldness of ignoring exceptions.

Drop it casually

“Changing the whole company’s rules over one incident? Hard cases make bad law, as they say.”
