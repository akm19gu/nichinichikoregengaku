Word of the day for July 8, 2028 (Day 646)

Brownian motion

Science

Category: Science科 学

In short

The ceaseless, random jiggling of tiny particles suspended in water. It became evidence that atoms and molecules really exist.

The story

In 1827 the British botanist Robert Brown watched under a microscope as tiny particles released from pollen grains kept jittering in water. It looked like a sign of life, but powdered stone showed the same motion. In 1905 Albert Einstein explained it as the result of invisible water molecules constantly bumping into the particles, and the French physicist Jean Perrin later confirmed it experimentally.

The lens

It’s often described as pollen moving, but what Brown saw wasn’t the pollen grains themselves but much smaller particles that came out of them. Confirming the invisible from the trembling of the visible was a superb piece of scientific reasoning.

Drop it casually

“Bumped from every side in the crowd and making no progress at all. Now I know how a particle in Brownian motion feels.”
