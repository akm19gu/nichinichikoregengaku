Harvard Computers

Space

Category: Space宇 宙

In short

The women who, from the late 19th century, did the calculations and classifications of star photographs at the Harvard College Observatory. Back then, “computers” meant people who computed.

The story

The observatory’s director, Edward Pickering, hired many women to examine the stars recorded on huge numbers of glass photographic plates. They were paid far less than men, yet they classified hundreds of thousands of stars and made major discoveries. Annie Jump Cannon developed the system of stellar classification still in use today.

The lens

Henrietta Swan Leavitt discovered the relationship between the brightness of certain variable stars and the period over which their brightness changes. It became a yardstick for measuring cosmic distances and opened the way to showing that the universe is vastly larger than anyone had imagined. The “computers,” whose names were seldom remembered, mapped the universe.

Drop it casually

“Counting stars before spreadsheets existed. I bow to the patience of the Harvard Computers.”
