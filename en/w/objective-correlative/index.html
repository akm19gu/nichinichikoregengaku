Word of the day for November 6, 2026 (Day 36)

Objective correlative

Literature

Category: Literature文 学

In short

Expressing an emotion not by naming it, but through a set of objects, situations or events that call it up.

The story

T. S. Eliot used the phrase in his 1919 essay “Hamlet and His Problems.” The only way to express emotion in art, he wrote, is to find a set of objects or a chain of events that will be the formula for that particular emotion. He went so far as to call Hamlet “an artistic failure,” because Hamlet’s feelings have no adequate object in the play.

The lens

Instead of writing “I was sad,” describe two cups of coffee left to go cold. The idea works just as well in letters and social media posts.

Drop it casually

“Instead of saying I missed them, I sent a photo of one lonely slipper by the door. Going full objective correlative.”
