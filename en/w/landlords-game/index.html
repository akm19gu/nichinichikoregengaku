The Landlord’s Game

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

An early-20th-century American board game that became the prototype of Monopoly. It was designed to show the evils of land monopoly.

The story

Elizabeth Magie, an American woman, patented it in 1904. A follower of the economist Henry George, who argued for taxing income from land, she designed it so players could experience how landlords monopolizing land made everyone else poorer. It also came with an alternative set of rules for sharing the wealth.

The lens

The game spread from player to player, picking up changes along the way, until a man named Charles Darrow sold it as Monopoly and it became a smash hit. It’s ironic that a game meant to teach the harms of monopoly became famous for the fun of bankrupting your opponents.

Drop it casually

“I lost everything at Monopoly. Apparently it started as the Landlord’s Game, meant to teach how scary monopolies are. Lesson learned.”
