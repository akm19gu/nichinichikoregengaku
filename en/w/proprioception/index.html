Proprioception

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

The sense that tells you where your arms and legs are and how they’re moving, even with your eyes closed. Sometimes called the sixth sense.

The story

Sensors in muscles, tendons, and joints constantly report the body’s position and movement to the brain. The physiologist Charles Sherrington popularized the name in 1906. The neurologist Oliver Sacks described a woman who lost this sense and could move her body only while watching it.

The lens

You can touch your nose with your eyes shut thanks to this sense. It isn’t counted among the five senses, yet without it even standing becomes hard. A reminder that some senses are too ordinary to notice.

Drop it casually

“I can get a glass to my mouth in the dark thanks to proprioception, apparently.”
