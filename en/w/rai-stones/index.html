Rai stones of Yap

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

Stone money from the Micronesian island of Yap. The biggest pieces are taller than a person.

The story

The limestone discs were quarried on islands in Palau, hundreds of kilometers away, and carried back by canoe. Large stones were never moved. When one changed hands, the community simply remembered who owned it now. One famous story tells of a stone that sank to the bottom of the sea in transit and still counted as wealth, because everyone agreed it existed.

The lens

The economist Milton Friedman used the stones to argue that money’s value lies not in the object but in shared belief. A system where ownership changes only in the ledger is often compared to bank deposits and cryptocurrencies.

Drop it casually

“My savings are just numbers in a bank’s records. Honestly, not that different from the rai stones of Yap.”
