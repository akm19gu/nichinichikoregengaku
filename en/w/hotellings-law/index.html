Hotelling’s law

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

Competing shops and products, fighting over customers, end up surprisingly similar in location and content.

The story

The American economist Harold Hotelling showed it in a 1929 paper. With two ice cream sellers on a beach, customers would be best served if they stood at opposite ends, but each edges toward the center to win a few more customers, until both end up side by side in the middle.

The lens

Convenience stores cluster outside stations, TV networks make similar shows, and parties’ platforms start to resemble each other. Sameness may come not from a lack of originality but from competition itself.

Drop it casually

“The new shopping street is wall-to-wall look-alike cafés. A textbook case of Hotelling’s law.”
