Apophatic theology

Religion & Myth

Category: Religion & Myth宗 教神 話

In short

A way of approaching God by saying what God is not, layering negation upon negation, instead of saying what God is.

The story

It rests on the idea that God so far exceeds human words and concepts that even calling God “good” or saying God “exists” would put limits on God. The writings of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, from around the late fifth or early sixth century, are the classic source, and they deeply shaped later mysticism.

The lens

You could see it as a way of tracing the outline of the unsayable by surrounding it with words from the outside. It’s tempting to set it next to Laozi’s “The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao.”

Drop it casually

“I can’t say what that dish tastes like. Not spicy, not sweet, not sour. I can only describe it apophatically.”
