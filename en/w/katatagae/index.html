Word of the day for June 21, 2028 (Day 629)

Katatagae

Religion & Myth

Category: Religion & Myth宗 教神 話

In short

In Heian-period Japan, when the direction of your destination was considered unlucky, you first spent the night somewhere in another direction to change your bearing before setting out.

The story

In Onmyōdō, the Japanese system of yin-yang divination, heading straight toward a direction where certain deities were traveling was thought to bring misfortune. So aristocrats would move to another place the night before, stay over, and set out having “changed direction.” In The Tale of Genji, the hero Genji meets the lady Utsusemi at a house he visits for exactly this reason.

The lens

It seems like a tiresome rule, but it also served as a pretext for visiting and staying over with people, leading to unexpected encounters. Taking the long way around for luck has a proper traditional name.

Drop it casually

“The meeting room is in an unlucky direction today, so I’ll go by way of the café. Wait, that’s katatagae.”
