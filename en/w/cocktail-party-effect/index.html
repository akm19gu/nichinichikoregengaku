Cocktail party effect

Psychology

Category: Psychology心 理

In short

Even in a noisy place, you can’t help picking out your own name or a topic that interests you.

The story

The British cognitive scientist Colin Cherry studied it in 1953. He played different speech to each ear and had listeners attend to only one. They remembered almost nothing of the other, yet they noticed things like the speaker’s voice changing from male to female. Later research showed that many people notice when their own name comes through the ear they were supposed to ignore.

The lens

Our attention works like a filter that picks out what we want to hear. Reproducing that selective listening is still a hard problem for hearing aids and speech recognition.

Drop it casually

“In a loud bar, the only words I caught from the next table were “changing jobs.” Cocktail party effect.”
