Chekhov’s gun

Literature

Category: Literature文 学

In short

Anything introduced in a story must matter later on.

The story

The Russian playwright Anton Chekhov expressed the idea several times in letters, and it is best known as: if a gun hangs on the wall in the first act, it must go off in a later one. An element that is never used is a promise to the audience that is never kept.

The lens

It is the ancestor of every satisfying payoff to a setup. In mysteries, though, writers deliberately hang guns that never fire to mislead the reader: the red herring.

Drop it casually

“That old key they kept showing in episode one? It’s definitely coming back. Chekhov’s gun.”
