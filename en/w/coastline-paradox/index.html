Coastline paradox

Geography & Earth

Category: Geography & Earth地 理地 球

In short

The finer the ruler you use to measure a coastline, the longer it gets, so it never settles on a single length.

The story

The British mathematician Lewis Fry Richardson noticed that figures for the lengths of borders and coastlines varied wildly from source to source. A 100-kilometer ruler skips over inlets, but as you go down to 1 kilometer, then 1 meter, you trace smaller and smaller wiggles and the length keeps growing. In 1967 the mathematician Benoit Mandelbrot took this up in a paper titled “How Long Is the Coast of Britain?”, leading toward his later theory of fractals.

The lens

Even length, a number that seems so objective, changes with how you measure. When you look at statistics or ratings, it’s worth checking the size of the ruler too.

Drop it casually

“How long is this island’s coastline? By the coastline paradox, that depends on your ruler.”
