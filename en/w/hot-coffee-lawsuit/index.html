The McDonald’s coffee case

Liebeck v. McDonald’s Restaurants

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

The 1990s American lawsuit by a woman who suffered severe burns from spilled McDonald’s coffee. For years it was a punch line about a lawsuit-crazy society.

The story

Stella Liebeck, 79, spilled coffee from a drive-through in her lap in a parked car and suffered burns severe enough to need skin grafts. She initially asked for about $20,000 to cover medical costs; the company offered $800. At trial it emerged that the company had received more than 700 burn complaints, and the jury awarded $2.7 million in punitive damages, roughly two days of the company’s coffee sales.

The lens

The award was later sharply reduced, and the case finally settled for an undisclosed amount. Yet only the story of a greedy old woman who struck it rich by spilling coffee spread through TV and newspapers. Behind an easy joke, untold facts may be hiding.

Drop it casually

“Before you laugh about the woman who got rich off spilled coffee, please read what the McDonald’s coffee case was actually about.”
