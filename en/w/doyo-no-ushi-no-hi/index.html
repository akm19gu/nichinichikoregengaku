Word of the day for July 27, 2027 (Day 299)

Doyō no Ushi no Hi

Food

Category: Food食

In short

The Day of the Ox in the midsummer doyō period of the old Japanese calendar. The custom of eating grilled eel on this day spread in the Edo period.

The story

A well-known story says an eel shop, struggling with poor summer sales, asked the inventor and writer Hiraga Gennai for help, and he told them to post a sign reading “Today is the Day of the Ox,” and business boomed. No solid record backs this up. The idea that eel helps with summer fatigue is far older: in the eighth-century poetry anthology Man’yōshū, the poet Ōtomo no Yakamochi teases a friend that eel is the cure for summer weight loss.

The lens

One theory is that eel sellers rode an older saying that on the Day of the Ox you should eat foods starting with “u,” as unagi, eel, does. Tradition and clever marketing have worked hand in hand for a long time.

Drop it casually

“Eel on the Day of the Ox might have been Hiraga Gennai’s marketing trick, or might not. I’m eating it either way.”
