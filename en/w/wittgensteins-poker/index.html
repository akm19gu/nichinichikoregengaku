Wittgenstein’s poker

Philosophy

Category: Philosophy哲 学思 考

In short

A famous ten-minute argument at a Cambridge philosophy meeting in 1946, in which Wittgenstein reportedly brandished a fireplace poker at Karl Popper.

The story

Karl Popper, invited to speak, clashed fiercely with Ludwig Wittgenstein, who held that philosophy has no genuine problems. According to Popper’s later account, Wittgenstein, poker in hand, demanded an example of a moral rule, and Popper replied, “Not to threaten visiting lecturers with pokers.” The accounts of those present contradict each other, and what really happened is still unclear.

The lens

A showdown between two of the 20th century’s leading philosophers comes down to us as a ten-minute event with conflicting testimony. It can also be read as a demonstration of how unreliable memory and eyewitness accounts are.

Drop it casually

“Everyone at yesterday’s meeting tells a different story about the argument. It’s Wittgenstein’s poker all over again.”
