Humorism

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

The medical theory, dating back to ancient Greece, that the body is made of four humors, blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile, and that illness comes when they fall out of balance.

The story

It began with physicians of the Hippocratic school in ancient Greece and was systematized by the Roman-era physician Galen, serving as the foundation of medicine in Europe and the Islamic world for well over a thousand years. Treatments to rebalance the humors, such as bloodletting, remained common into the 19th century.

The lens

The theory lives on in our vocabulary. “Humor” originally meant a bodily fluid before coming to mean temperament and mood, and “melancholy” comes from the Greek for black bile. We still describe our moods in the language of ancient medicine.

Drop it casually

“Feeling melancholy today. In humorism terms, it’s a black bile day.”
