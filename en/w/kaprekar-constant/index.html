Kaprekar’s constant

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

6174, the number that nearly every four-digit number reaches if you repeatedly arrange its digits into the largest and smallest numbers and subtract.

The story

D. R. Kaprekar, a schoolteacher of mathematics in India, published it in 1949. Take 3524: 5432 − 2345 = 3087, 8730 − 0378 = 8352, 8532 − 2358 = 6174. From 6174 you get 7641 − 1467 = 6174, the same number forever. Unless all four digits are the same, as in 1111, you reach 6174 within seven steps from any start.

The lens

In his lifetime, Kaprekar was often dismissed by professional mathematicians as a dabbler in number games. He never stopped playing with numbers, and today the numbers and constant bearing his name delight math lovers around the world.

Drop it casually

“Wherever the meeting starts, it ends at the same conclusion. Our company’s Kaprekar constant is “we’ll consider it positively.””
