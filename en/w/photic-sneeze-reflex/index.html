Word of the day for January 24, 2028 (Day 480)

Photic sneeze reflex

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

Sneezing when you step suddenly from darkness into bright light.

The story

A book attributed to Aristotle already asks why the heat of the sun makes people sneeze. In the 17th century, Francis Bacon noticed that closing his eyes in the sun stopped the sneezing and concluded that light entering the eyes was the cause. Some people have it and some don’t, and it runs in families. In English it’s also called ACHOO syndrome, a backronym that echoes the sound of a sneeze.

The lens

One theory is that the nerves carrying signals from the eyes and from the nose get their wires crossed in the brain. Even a single sneeze reveals the quirks of the body’s wiring.

Drop it casually

“I sneezed the moment we came out of the tunnel. Photic sneeze reflex. Definitely not a cold.”
