Knox’s Decalogue

Literature

Category: Literature文 学

In short

Ten rules that detective fiction writers were supposed to follow.

The story

The English priest and mystery writer Ronald Knox published them in 1929. They include “The criminal must be someone mentioned in the early part of the story,” “No accident must ever help the detective, nor must he ever have an unaccountable intuition which proves to be right,” and “Twin brothers, and doubles generally, must not appear unless we have been duly prepared for them,” as well as one rule that reflects the prejudices of the time.

The lens

They were meant to keep the battle of wits between reader and author fair, but many classics broke them on purpose to surprise readers. Knowing the rules makes the shock of seeing them broken all the greater.

Drop it casually

“The culprit turns out to be a twin brother introduced on the last page? Knox’s Decalogue would like a word.”
