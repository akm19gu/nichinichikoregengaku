Architecture without architects

Architecture & Cities

Category: Architecture & Cities建 築都 市

In short

Buildings handed down through generations by local people, rather than designed by professional architects, shaped to fit the climate, materials, and way of life.

The story

In 1964 the Austrian-born architect Bernard Rudofsky held an exhibition of this name at the Museum of Modern Art in New York. Photos of cave dwellings, villages clinging to cliffs, and portable homes from around the world revealed the wisdom and beauty of anonymous builders, and the show made a big impact.

The lens

The steep thatched gasshō-zukuri farmhouses of Shirakawa-go in Japan belong to this family too. No single person designed them, yet generations of trial and error produced the form best suited to the place.

Drop it casually

“The thatched farmhouses of Shirakawa-go are masterpieces of architecture without architects. Snow-country wisdom turned straight into form.”
