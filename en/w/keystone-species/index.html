Keystone species

Science

Category: Science科 学

In short

A species that, even in small numbers, holds the structure of an entire ecosystem together.

The story

The ecologist Robert Paine proposed the idea in 1969. In tide pools on the Pacific coast of North America, he removed a predatory starfish. The mussels it used to eat multiplied out of control, crowded out other creatures, and the tide pools quickly lost their diversity. A keystone is the stone at the top of an arch that holds the rest in place.

The lens

People often tell how reintroducing wolves to Yellowstone changed the elks’ behavior and, in turn, the plants and even the rivers, though how large that effect was is debated. The term is tempting for the colleague whose absence brings everything to a halt.

Drop it casually

“When our office manager is out, the whole department stops. A true keystone species.”
