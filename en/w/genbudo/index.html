Genbudō Cave

Geography & Earth

Category: Geography & Earth地 理地 球

In short

A cave in Toyooka, Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, lined with hexagonal stone columns. The Japanese word for basalt, genbugan, comes from its name.

The story

About 1.6 million years ago, volcanic lava shrank as it cooled and hardened, cracking into regular column-shaped rock. An Edo-period Confucian scholar named the cave after Genbu, the Black Tortoise, a legendary beast whose shell the pattern resembled. In 1884 the geologist Bunjirō Kotō gave the rock type the name genbugan, “Genbu rock.”

The lens

In 1926 the geophysicist Motonori Matuyama found that rocks at Genbudō were magnetized in the opposite direction from today’s field, which led to the great discovery that Earth’s magnetic field reverses. One cave is tied both to the name of a rock and to solving a puzzle of Earth’s history.

Drop it casually

“The Japanese word for basalt comes from a cave in Hyōgo. Even textbook vocabulary has a hometown.”
