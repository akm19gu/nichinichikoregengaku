Sham surgery

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

A comparison procedure used to test whether an operation works, in which patients are anesthetized and cut but the actual treatment isn’t performed.

The story

In 2002 a US study compared a widely performed arthroscopic surgery for knee arthritis with a sham procedure that involved only skin incisions. The patients weren’t told which they had received. Both groups reported the same improvement in pain.

The lens

The experience of having had surgery can work on the body much like a placebo pill. Separating “it feels like it worked” from “it works” takes rigorous comparisons like this. Because they involve deceiving patients, though, they’re also hotly debated on ethical grounds.

Drop it casually

“I started taking expensive supplements and I feel great. But knowing about sham surgery studies, I wonder if it’s all in my head.”
