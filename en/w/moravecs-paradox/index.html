Moravec’s paradox

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The paradox that reasoning and calculation, hard for humans, are easy for computers, while the perception and movement humans do effortlessly are extremely hard for them.

The story

The roboticist Hans Moravec and others pointed it out in the 1980s. Moravec wrote that it is comparatively easy to make computers perform at adult level on intelligence tests or games like checkers, but difficult or impossible to give them the perception and mobility of a one-year-old.

The lens

Walking, grasping, recognizing faces: the things we do without thinking are tremendous abilities honed by long evolution. Our yardstick for what’s easy and what’s hard is built around humans.

Drop it casually

“Robots solve hard math instantly but still struggle to fold laundry. Moravec’s paradox.”
