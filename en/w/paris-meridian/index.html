Word of the day for July 14, 2028 (Day 652)

Paris meridian

Geography & Earth

Category: Geography & Earth地 理地 球

In short

The line of longitude through the Paris Observatory. For a long time France used it, not Greenwich, as its reference for longitude.

The story

At an international conference in 1884, the meridian through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich in England was chosen as the world’s reference for longitude. France voted against it and kept using Paris time for years. When France finally changed its time in 1911, the law avoided naming Greenwich, defining legal time instead as “Paris mean time, retarded by nine minutes and twenty-one seconds.”

The lens

In effect matching Greenwich while refusing to write its name: national pride shows up even in the wording of laws. In Paris today, small bronze medallions set into the streets still trace the meridian.

Drop it casually

“Even if I concede, I won’t write their name. I’d like at least as much stubbornness as the Paris meridian law.”
