The beriberi controversy

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

A long dispute in Meiji-era Japan over the cause of and cure for beriberi, between a navy that changed its diet and army doctors who clung to a germ theory.

The story

At the time, beriberi was a mysterious disease afflicting huge numbers of soldiers. The navy surgeon Takaki Kanehiro suspected diet and dramatically reduced beriberi in the navy by adding bread and barley to meals centered on white rice. Meanwhile army doctors, including Mori Rintarō, better known as the novelist Mori Ōgai, favored the theory that beriberi was caused by germs and kept the white-rice diet.

The lens

In the Russo-Japanese War, more than 250,000 army soldiers are said to have contracted beriberi, and some 27,000 died. Beriberi was later found to be caused by a lack of vitamin B1, which white rice alone doesn’t supply. The controversy still asks us to stay humble before results, rather than prestigious theories.

Drop it casually

“Are we clinging to theory and ignoring the results right in front of us? The lesson of the beriberi controversy applies in meeting rooms too.”
