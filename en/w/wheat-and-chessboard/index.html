Wheat and chessboard problem

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

Put one grain of wheat on the first square of a chessboard, two on the next, four on the next, doubling each time, and by the last square you have an unimaginable amount.

The story

In an old Indian legend, the sage who invented chess is asked by the king what reward he wants, and he asks for wheat counted this way. The king laughs at such a modest request, but the 64 squares add up to more than 18 quintillion grains, something like a thousand years of today’s world wheat harvest. Japan has a similar tale of Sorori Shinzaemon, a jester to the warlord Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who asked for rice doubled day by day.

The lens

Things that double barely register at first, then suddenly get out of hand. Recall this story when you think about the spread of an epidemic or interest piling up on a debt, and the menace of exponential growth becomes real.

Drop it casually

“My plan to double my study time every day, starting from one minute? By the chessboard math, I run out of day on day 12.”
