Limey

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

A nickname, mainly American, for British sailors and British people, from the lime juice drunk aboard ship to prevent scurvy.

The story

On long voyages, many sailors died of scurvy, caused by a lack of vitamin C. In 1747 the Royal Navy surgeon James Lind divided patients into groups to compare treatments and found that oranges and lemons worked. It’s considered one of the earliest controlled trials. The Royal Navy later began issuing citrus juice.

The lens

In the 19th century, however, the navy switched from lemons to more readily available West Indian limes. Limes contain less vitamin C, and scurvy returned on Arctic expeditions and elsewhere. The lime that gave the nickname wasn’t actually very effective.

Drop it casually

“Limey comes from lime. I love the punch line that it was really the lemons that worked.”
