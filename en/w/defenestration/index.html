Defenestration

History

Category: History歴 史

In short

The act of throwing someone out of a window. Yes, there is a word just for that.

The story

From Latin de (out of) and fenestra (window). Prague made it famous: in 1419 and again in 1618, political and religious quarrels ended with officials being thrown from windows. The 1618 incident helped spark the Thirty Years’ War. All three men thrown from the castle that year survived, and one popular story says a pile of manure broke their fall.

The lens

Today the word is also used for pushing someone out of a job or a seat of power. It is a reminder that big turning points in history sometimes start with something surprisingly physical.

Drop it casually

“One comment in that meeting and he was off the project. A textbook corporate defenestration.”
