Yagi–Uda antenna

Technology

Category: Technology技 術発 明

In short

An antenna that efficiently picks up radio waves from a particular direction, developed in the 1920s by Hidetsugu Yagi and Shintarō Uda at Tōhoku Imperial University. It’s still used for TV antennas and more.

The story

The antenna drew little attention in Japan, but its value was recognized in the West, and in World War II it was used in radar and other systems. A story goes that in 1942, after occupying Singapore, Japanese forces found the word “YAGI” in British documents and had no idea what it meant, and were astonished to learn from a prisoner that it was a Japanese name.

The lens

Sometimes the inventors’ own country fails to see the value of an invention. The eye for new things may belong not to where something was born but to whoever thinks about how to use it. Spot an antenna on a rooftop and you might remember this story.

Drop it casually

“The TV antenna on the roof is a Yagi antenna. A Japanese invention that was appreciated abroad first.”
