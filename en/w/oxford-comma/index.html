Word of the day for January 6, 2028 (Day 462)

Oxford comma

Language

Category: Language言 葉

In short

The comma before the final “and” or “or” in a list of three or more items. Whether to use it has been argued over for ages.

The story

It’s named for Oxford University Press, which has traditionally used it. In 2017 in Maine, a single missing one decided a court case. A state law listed tasks exempt from overtime pay without a comma before the last item, raising the question of whether dairy delivery drivers were covered, and the court sided with the drivers. The next year the company settled for about $5 million.

The lens

A single dot of punctuation can be worth millions. Legal and contract language sometimes reads awkwardly redundant precisely to stamp out ambiguities like this one by one.

Drop it casually

“My boss obsesses over every comma in a contract. Since I learned about the Oxford comma case, I think he’s right.”
