Vulcan

Space

Category: Space宇 宙

In short

A phantom planet, predicted in the 19th century to orbit closer to the sun than Mercury, that many people searched for.

The story

In 1846 the French astronomer Urbain Le Verrier used irregularities in Uranus’s orbit to calculate the position of an unknown planet, leading to the discovery of Neptune. He used the same method to explain a slight anomaly in Mercury’s orbit, proposing another planet farther in. It was named Vulcan after the Roman god of fire, and sightings were reported one after another, but none were ever confirmed.

The lens

In 1915, Einstein’s general theory of relativity explained Mercury’s orbit precisely without any unknown planet. The same method once found a new planet and once forced a rethink of the theory itself. Vulcan was a phantom that reflected the limits of an old theory.

Drop it casually

“Are we blaming numbers that don’t add up on something invisible? Before we go hunting for Vulcan, let’s question our assumptions.”
