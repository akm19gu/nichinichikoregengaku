Parable of the broken window

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

A short story exposing the mistake of thinking that a broken window is good for the economy because it gives the glazier work.

The story

The French economist Frédéric Bastiat told it in his 1850 essay “That Which Is Seen, and That Which Is Not Seen.” The money spent on fixing the window is visible as the glazier’s income. But if the window had not been broken, the shopkeeper might have spent the same money on a pair of shoes. Bastiat warned against forgetting that unseen loss.

The lens

Whenever disaster rebuilding or tear-down-and-rebuild projects are praised for their economic boost, it reminds you to ask what else the money could have done. It has nothing to do with the “broken windows theory” of policing, so don’t mix them up.

Drop it casually

“He says dropping and replacing his phone “helped the economy.” Someone tell him the parable of the broken window.”
