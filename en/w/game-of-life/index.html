Game of Life

Conway’s Game of Life

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

A virtual ecosystem run by a few simple rules, in which squares on a grid live or die depending on the state of their neighbors.

The story

The British mathematician John Horton Conway devised it, and it became a craze after Martin Gardner described it in his magazine column in 1970. The rules: a live cell survives if it has two or three live neighbors, and an empty cell comes alive if it has exactly three. From that alone emerge astonishingly varied patterns, such as “gliders” that keep their shape while traveling across the grid.

The lens

It was later shown that combinations of these rules can build a machine capable of any computation. The idea that complexity can arise from simple rules has become an important clue for thinking about life and society.

Drop it casually

“The rules are simple, yet something different happens every day. Is my office running the Game of Life?”
