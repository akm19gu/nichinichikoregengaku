Fata Morgana

Geography & Earth

Category: Geography & Earth地 理地 球

In short

A kind of mirage, often seen over the sea, in which distant scenery stretches upward or floats, as if castles or cities hung in the air.

The story

The name comes from the Italian for Morgan le Fay, the sorceress of Arthurian legend. In the Strait of Messina, phantom castles appearing over the sea were said to be illusions she conjured to lure sailors. In fact it happens when light bends through layers of air at different temperatures.

The lens

In Japan, the mirages of Uozu on Toyama Bay are famous. The Chinese and Japanese word for mirage means “a clam’s tower,” from the belief that a giant clam breathed out pavilions in the air. East and West alike, people have sensed some will behind cities floating in the sky.

Drop it casually

“I saw a puddle shimmering at the end of the road on a hot day. Not quite a Fata Morgana, but part of the mirage family.”
