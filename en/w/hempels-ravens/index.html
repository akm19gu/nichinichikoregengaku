Hempel’s ravens

Philosophy

Category: Philosophy哲 学思 考

In short

The strange paradox that the hypothesis “all ravens are black” seems to be confirmed every time you find something non-black that isn’t a raven.

The story

The German-born philosopher Carl Hempel presented it in the 1940s. “All ravens are black” is logically equivalent to “everything that isn’t black isn’t a raven.” So every white shoe or red apple you come across would seem to support your hypothesis about ravens.

The lens

Mocked as a way to do ornithology without leaving your room, the paradox sharply raises the question of what counts as evidence. Collecting lots of examples isn’t the same as testing a hypothesis.

Drop it casually

“Every red apple I see confirms that ravens are black? Hempel’s ravens never stop being weird.”
