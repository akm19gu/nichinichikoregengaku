Prussian blue

Art & Film

Category: Art & Film芸 術

In short

A synthetic blue pigment discovered by accident in early-18th-century Berlin. In Japan it was called bero-ai, Berlin indigo, and it transformed the blues of ukiyo-e prints.

The story

In the early 1700s a Berlin dyemaker trying to make a red pigment reportedly got a vivid blue instead, thanks to an impurity in his materials. Earlier blues had been made from costly minerals and the like, but this one was relatively cheap and resisted fading. It reached Edo-period Japan and supplied the deep blues of Katsushika Hokusai’s Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji.

The lens

A color born from a failed experiment crossed the oceans and dyed the great wave of The Great Wave off Kanagawa. Trace the colors of famous paintings back and you meet unexpected stories of chemistry.

Drop it casually

“The blue in Hokusai’s wave is a color born from a botched batch in Berlin. What a journey Prussian blue has had.”
