Bradley effect

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

When a minority candidate leads in the polls but gets noticeably fewer votes in the actual election.

The story

In the 1982 California governor’s race, Tom Bradley, the Black mayor of Los Angeles, led in the polls but narrowly lost. One explanation was that some respondents gave answers that didn’t match their true intentions because they didn’t want to seem to be choosing on the basis of race.

The lens

In polls, people tend to give the answer considered desirable. Researchers disagree, though, about whether the effect really existed and whether it shows up in recent elections. Behind the numbers, it’s worth imagining the feelings of the people answering.

Drop it casually

“Everyone said in the survey they’d love to come, and nobody showed up. A kind of Bradley effect.”
