Leidenfrost effect

Science

Category: Science科 学

In short

Drop water onto a red-hot pan and, instead of boiling away at once, the drops bead up and skitter around.

The story

The German physician Johann Gottlob Leidenfrost described it in detail in 1756. The bottom of the drop vaporizes instantly, forming a thin layer of steam that cushions the drop and insulates it, so the drop actually lasts longer.

The lens

The cook’s trick of checking whether a pan is hot enough by watching whether water beads and dances uses this effect. Sometimes being too hot is what lets something hold out longer.

Drop it casually

“In front of my furious boss, I somehow stayed calm. Maybe a Leidenfrost layer was protecting me.”
