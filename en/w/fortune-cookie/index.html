Fortune cookie

Food

Category: Food食

In short

The crisp cookie with a fortune inside served after meals at Chinese restaurants in America. Its roots, it turns out, are probably Japanese.

The story

Near Kyoto’s Fushimi Inari Shrine and elsewhere, bakers have long made tsujiura senbei, folded crackers with a paper fortune tucked inside. Japanese immigrants are thought to have brought them to America in the early 20th century, serving them at places like the Japanese Tea Garden in San Francisco. Research by the Japanese scholar Yasuko Nakamachi helped confirm these roots.

The lens

When Japanese Americans were forced into internment camps during World War II, Chinese American makers are said to have taken over production, and the cookie settled in as a “Chinese” treat. A whole immigrant history is folded into one little cookie.

Drop it casually

“Fortune cookies trace back to fortune crackers from Kyoto. Now they taste like a shrine visit to me.”
