Dōjima Rice Exchange

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

A rice exchange in Edo-period Osaka, officially recognized by the shogunate in 1730, considered the world’s first organized futures market.

The story

In the Edo period, feudal lords shipped their tax rice to Osaka to turn it into money, and rice prices drove the whole economy. At Dōjima, traders bought and sold not only physical rice but future rice prices on paper, and in the time of the shogun Tokugawa Yoshimune the shogunate gave the market official sanction.

The lens

It reportedly already had the basics of modern futures trading, such as margin deposits and settling only the price difference in cash at the end. That was more than a century before the Chicago Board of Trade. Edo merchants were at the cutting edge of world finance.

Drop it casually

“I thought futures trading was a Western invention. The Dōjima Rice Exchange beat Chicago by more than a century.”
