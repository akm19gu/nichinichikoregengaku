Repugnant conclusion

Philosophy

Category: Philosophy哲 学思 考

In short

The problem that maximizing total happiness seems to imply that a huge population of lives barely worth living is better than a smaller, flourishing one.

The story

The British philosopher Derek Parfit named it in his 1984 book Reasons and Persons. Compare a small world of very happy people with a vast world of people just scraping by, and the latter can come out ahead in total happiness. Parfit himself kept searching for a theory that could avoid the conclusion.

The lens

It’s still one of the unsolved puzzles in the ethics of population and future generations. It makes you ask where the idea that “more is better” parts ways with our intuitions.

Drop it casually

“Pump out lots of thin articles, or publish a few rich ones? It’s like a choice out of the repugnant conclusion.”
