Word of the day for March 16, 2028 (Day 532)

Eel’s bed

unagi no nedoko

Architecture & Cities

Category: Architecture & Cities建 築都 市

In short

A Japanese expression for a long, narrow lot or building with a narrow frontage and great depth, like the townhouses of Kyoto.

The story

A popular explanation says taxes were levied by the width of the frontage, so people kept frontages narrow to save money. But firm evidence is thin, and another view holds that the blocks were divided into thin strips so that many households could share space along streets good for business. In the middle of these long townhouses there is often a small courtyard garden to let in light and air.

The lens

A plausible tax-dodging story is tempting to believe. But the shape of a town comes not from a single reason but from layers of commerce, daily life, and history. The tidier the explanation, the more worth doubting.

Drop it casually

“The new shop has a narrow front and goes way back. A true eel’s bed. The little courtyard garden in the back was lovely.”
