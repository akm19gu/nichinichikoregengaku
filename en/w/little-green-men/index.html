Word of the day for May 20, 2028 (Day 597)

Little Green Men

LGM-1

Space

Category: Space宇 宙

In short

The joking provisional name given to the first pulsar, a celestial object emitting perfectly regular radio pulses, discovered in 1967.

The story

Jocelyn Bell, a graduate student at the University of Cambridge, spotted a signal in radio telescope data that repeated precisely about every 1.3 seconds. It was so regular that, half joking that it might be aliens, the team called it LGM-1. It turned out to be a neutron star, an ultra-dense star spinning rapidly.

The lens

The 1974 Nobel Prize in Physics for this discovery went to her supervisor and a colleague, not to Bell herself. Yet when she later won a different prize worth $3 million, she donated all of it to help students with few opportunities to study physics. The person who found the signal from space also lit a path for the next generation.

Drop it casually

“My boss’s email arrives at exactly the same minute every morning. Like Little Green Men, there must be some mechanism behind it.”
