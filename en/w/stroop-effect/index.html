Word of the day for August 4, 2027 (Day 307)

Stroop effect

Psychology

Category: Psychology心 理

In short

When the word “red” is printed in blue ink, it takes longer to name the ink color.

The story

The American psychologist John Ridley Stroop reported it in 1935. Reading is so automatic that the meaning of a word pushes in even when you don’t want it to, and it collides with the effort to name the color. Simple as the task is, it’s still used around the world in experiments on attention.

The lens

A thoroughly practiced action starts up on its own even when you try to stop it. Reaching for your phone when you’ve sworn not to may work in a similar way.

Drop it casually

“The word STOP was painted in green and I froze for a second. Stroop effect.”
