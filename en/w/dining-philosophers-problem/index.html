Dining philosophers problem

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

Five philosophers at a round table each need the two forks they share with their neighbors to eat, and they can end up all stuck.

The story

The Dutch computer scientist Edsger Dijkstra devised a version around 1965 as an exam problem for students, and the British computer scientist Tony Hoare is credited with recasting it as today’s story. If everyone picks up their left fork at the same time, each waits forever for the right fork to free up, and no one eats. It became the classic illustration of deadlock, where multiple processes competing for the same resources grind to a halt.

The lens

No one is at fault, yet if everyone holds what they have and waits, the whole system stops. The same structure lurks in meetings where approvals go around in circles and in gridlocked intersections.

Drop it casually

“The director is waiting on the manager’s slides, the manager is waiting on the director’s decision. Like the dining philosophers, everyone’s stuck.”
