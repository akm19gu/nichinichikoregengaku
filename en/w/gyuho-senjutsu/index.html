Ox-walk tactic

gyūho senjutsu

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

A form of resistance in Japan’s parliament in which lawmakers walk to the ballot box deliberately slowly, like oxen, to stall a vote.

The story

In Japan’s Diet, important bills are sometimes voted on with members walking one by one to the rostrum to cast their ballots. Opposition parties in the minority would slow this walk to an extreme to buy time and delay a bill’s passage. In 1992, over a bill opening the way for Japan’s Self-Defense Forces to join overseas peacekeeping missions, the upper house saw a marathon of ox-walking.

The lens

However much time it takes, the vote is usually decided by numbers in the end. Even so, the minority used the ox-walk to show the weight of its opposition in visible form. It’s a uniquely Japanese parliamentary scene that makes you think about what deliberation time and majority rule mean.

Drop it casually

“I’m progressing on this task I don’t want to do with the ox-walk tactic. The deadline will still come.”
