Schelling point

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The “obvious answer” that people who can’t talk to each other naturally converge on by trying to read each other’s minds.

The story

The American economist Thomas Schelling discussed it in a 1960 book. He asked students: you have to meet someone in New York tomorrow, but you haven’t agreed on a place or time. Where and when do you go? Many answered: Grand Central Station, at noon. Schelling later won the 2005 Nobel Prize in economics for his analysis of conflict and cooperation.

The lens

The usual meeting spot, or a negotiated price settling on a round number, works the same way. People are always searching for a shared landmark that lets them understand each other without words.

Drop it casually

“I can’t reach her, but she’s probably under the big clock by the ticket gates. That’s our Schelling point.”
