God’s number

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

The minimum number of moves needed to solve a Rubik’s Cube from any scrambled position. The answer is 20.

The story

A Rubik’s Cube has about 43 quintillion possible arrangements. Mathematicians and programmers spent years whittling down the upper bound, and in 2010, using enormous computing time donated by Google, they showed that every position can be solved in 20 moves or fewer, and that some positions need exactly 20. The name comes from the idea that an all-knowing God would always know the shortest solution.

The lens

The methods used in human speedcubing competitions take far more than 20 moves. That a shortest answer exists and that a person can find it are entirely different matters.

Drop it casually

“The handover docs are a mess, but like God’s number, there must be a path to fix them in 20 moves somewhere.”
