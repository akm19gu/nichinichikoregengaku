South Sea Bubble

History

Category: History歴 史

In short

A spectacular boom and crash of shares in Britain in 1720, and the classic example of a speculative bubble.

The story

The South Sea Company held a monopoly on trade with South America. Fueled by hopes that had little to do with its actual profits, its share price multiplied several times within about six months, then collapsed. Isaac Newton was among the losers and is said to have remarked that he could calculate the motions of the heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people. The quote itself may have been invented later.

The lens

Newton reportedly sold early at a profit, watched the price keep climbing, bought back in, and lost heavily. It is the classic lesson that clever people are especially prone to believing they will know when to get out.

Drop it casually

“Every time I hear about the hot new investment, I remember that even Newton got burned in the South Sea Bubble.”
