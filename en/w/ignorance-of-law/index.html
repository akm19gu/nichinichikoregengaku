Ignorance of the law is no excuse

ignorantia juris non excusat

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

The ancient legal principle that not knowing a law doesn’t excuse breaking it.

The story

The idea goes back to Roman law and has been passed down as a Latin maxim. If “I didn’t know” were accepted, anyone could escape punishment just by saying it. Many legal systems, including Japan’s penal code, state that ignorance of the law does not mean there was no intent to commit a crime.

The lens

Yet modern laws and regulations are so vast that not even experts can know them all. If people are held responsible even without knowing, those who make the laws have a duty to communicate them clearly too. The principle raises that question as well.

Drop it casually

“Saying I didn’t know the rule won’t fly. Ignorance of the law is no excuse. Time to actually read the terms of service.”
