Word of the day for February 28, 2028 (Day 515)

Carrington Event

Space

Category: Space宇 宙

In short

The 1859 solar storm, among the largest on record. Auroras were seen worldwide and telegraph systems went haywire.

The story

On September 1, 1859, the British astronomer Richard Carrington observed a dazzling flash on the surface of the sun. The next day a powerful solar storm reached Earth, and auroras were seen as far as the Caribbean and Hawaii. Strong currents surged through telegraph lines, sparks flew and scorched the paper, and some operators found they could send messages with their batteries disconnected.

The lens

If a storm of the same size hit today, it could do serious damage to satellites, power grids, and the internet. The sparks of telegraphs more than 150 years ago are a warning to a society that depends entirely on electricity.

Drop it casually

“I keep one paper map for the day phones and power go out. Preparing for the next Carrington Event.”
