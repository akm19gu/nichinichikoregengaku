Tragedy of the commons

Society

Category: Society社 会

In short

The paradox that when everyone acts rationally for themselves, a shared resource gets used up.

The story

The biologist Garrett Hardin made it famous in a 1968 article in Science. On a pasture where anyone can graze sheep, the gain from adding one more animal is yours, while the cost of thinner grass is shared by all, so eventually the pasture is ruined. The argument goes back to the 19th-century economist William Forster Lloyd.

The lens

The political scientist Elinor Ostrom later documented many communities that kept shared resources healthy for centuries with rules of their own, and in 2009 she won the Nobel Prize in economics. The sequel, “the tragedy isn’t inevitable,” comes as part of the set.

Drop it casually

“The office fridge turns into chaos every time. Tragedy of the commons. We need Ostrom-style house rules.”
