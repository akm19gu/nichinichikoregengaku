Trompe-l’œil

Art & Film

Category: Art & Film芸 術

In short

Painting so realistic and three-dimensional that it fools the eye into thinking it is real.

The story

French for “deceives the eye.” The Roman writer Pliny tells of a contest between two Greek painters. Zeuxis painted grapes so real that birds flew down to peck at them. Then he asked his rival Parrhasius to pull back the curtain covering his picture, and realized the curtain itself was painted. Zeuxis conceded: he had fooled birds, but Parrhasius had fooled a painter.

The lens

The technique flourished where painting meets architecture, in fake domes on flat ceilings and windows painted on walls. Today’s 3D street art is its descendant.

Drop it casually

“Wait, that window is painted on the wall? I fell for the trompe-l’œil completely.”
