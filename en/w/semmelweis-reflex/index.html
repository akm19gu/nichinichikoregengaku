Semmelweis reflex

Medicine & Body

Category: Medicine & Body医 学か ら だ

In short

The knee-jerk rejection of new evidence because it clashes with established beliefs or habits.

The story

In the 1840s the Viennese obstetrician Ignaz Semmelweis concluded that doctors who went straight from dissections to deliveries without washing their hands were spreading childbed fever. He made them wash in chlorinated water, and deaths fell sharply. But the medical establishment refused to accept that doctors’ own hands carried disease. His ideas were dismissed, and he died in an asylum.

The lens

Germ theory was only widely accepted after his death, in the era of Pasteur and Lister. It is the story of a man who was right and never rewarded, and also a handy word for checking whether you are reacting rather than thinking.

Drop it casually

“They shot down the new process without even hearing it out. Pure Semmelweis reflex.”
