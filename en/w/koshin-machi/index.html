Word of the day for June 2, 2028 (Day 610)

Kōshin vigil

kōshin-machi

Religion & Myth

Category: Religion & Myth宗 教神 話

In short

A Japanese custom of staying awake all night on the Kōshin day that comes once every 60 days, so the worms in your body can’t rise to heaven and report your misdeeds.

The story

According to Taoist belief, three worms called sanshi live in the human body. On the night of Kōshin they slip out while you sleep, report your sins to the god of heaven, and shorten your life. So people gathered to eat, drink, and talk, staying up all night. The custom was practiced by Heian-period aristocrats and spread to commoners in the Edo period.

The lens

Staying up to prevent tattling turned into a social occasion where neighbors gathered and had fun. The Kōshin mounds found across Japan and the statues of the three monkeys, “see no evil, speak no evil, hear no evil,” are closely tied to this belief.

Drop it casually

“An all-night gaming session is a modern Kōshin vigil. Those three worms must be exasperated with us.”
