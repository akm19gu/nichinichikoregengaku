Russell’s teapot

Philosophy

Category: Philosophy哲 学思 考

In short

The analogy that nobody can disprove a claim that a tiny teapot orbits the sun between Earth and Mars, but that’s no reason to believe it.

The story

The British philosopher Bertrand Russell made the argument in a 1952 piece written for a magazine but never published in his lifetime. If someone says a teapot too small to be seen by any telescope is orbiting the sun, the fact that it can’t be disproved is no ground for believing it. The burden of proof lies with whoever makes the claim, he argued.

The lens

“You can’t prove it isn’t there” is not evidence that it is. When you meet a claim hiding behind the fact that it can’t be disproved, this is the teapot to quietly bring out.

Drop it casually

“Ghosts exist because you can’t prove they don’t? That’s Russell’s teapot.”
