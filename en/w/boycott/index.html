Word of the day for June 2, 2027 (Day 244)

Boycott

Language

Category: Language言 葉

In short

Collectively cutting off all dealings with a person, company, or product as a form of protest.

The story

In 1880, Charles Boycott, an Englishman working as a land agent in Ireland, rejected tenant farmers’ demands for lower rents. At the urging of the Land League, the local people stopped dealing with him altogether. His workers left, shops wouldn’t sell to him, and even his mail stopped coming. Newspapers spread the story far and wide, and his name became the word for collectively shunning someone.

The lens

Showing your will not through violence but by refusing to take part went on to be used by protest movements around the world. Sometimes one person’s name lives on in the language like this.

Drop it casually

“Unfair price hikes get answered with a boycott. Mr. Boycott himself surely never expected his name to stick around.”
