Modulor

Architecture & Cities

Category: Architecture & Cities建 築都 市

In short

A system of proportions for buildings and furniture devised by the architect Le Corbusier, based on human body measurements and the golden ratio.

The story

Le Corbusier built a series of measurements for ceilings, windows, and furniture starting from a man 183 centimeters tall and the 226 centimeters he reaches with a raised hand. He originally based it on a height of 175 centimeters but changed it to 183 in 1946. His reason: in English detective novels, the good-looking men, policemen for instance, are always six feet tall.

The lens

It’s charming that a standard which changed world architecture had a taste in detective fiction behind it. It also raises the problem that treating one body type as the standard human makes many people who don’t fit it invisible.

Drop it casually

“I can’t reach the top shelf. The Modulor assumes you’re six feet tall. Designed for detective-novel policemen.”
