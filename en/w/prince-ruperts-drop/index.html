Prince Rupert’s drop

Science

Category: Science科 学

In short

A tadpole-shaped glass bead made by dripping molten glass into cold water. Its head survives a hammer blow, but snap the tip of its tail and the whole thing shatters instantly.

The story

The name comes from Prince Rupert of the Rhine, who presented them to King Charles II of England in the 17th century, after which they were studied at the Royal Society in London. The rapidly cooled surface tries to shrink hard while the inside is left stretched, so the surface is extremely strong, but a crack anywhere releases all the stored stress at once.

The lens

Strength and fragility live together in one object. It’s tempting to use it as a metaphor for an organization that looks rock-solid but can collapse from one unexpected point.

Drop it casually

“That team looks unbeatable from outside, but one person leaving could shatter it. Like a Prince Rupert’s drop.”
