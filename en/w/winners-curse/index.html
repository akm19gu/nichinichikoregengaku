Winner’s curse

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

The phenomenon that the winner of an auction has often paid more than the thing is really worth.

The story

In 1971, engineers at an American oil company wrote a paper on bidding for drilling rights in the Gulf of Mexico that popularized the term. Estimates of how much oil lies underground come with errors, and when many companies bid, the one that overestimated most is likeliest to win. So the companies that “win” tend to be the ones that lose money.

The lens

Bidding wars for hot properties, star athletes’ transfers, corporate takeovers. Right after winning a fierce contest is exactly when to ask whether you only won because you were the most optimistic.

Drop it casually

“I won the online auction. But it may be the winner’s curse. Now that I’ve calmed down, I paid way too much.”
