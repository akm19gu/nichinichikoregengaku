Word of the day for June 14, 2028 (Day 622)

Herostratic fame

History

Category: History歴 史

In short

Fame achieved through a misdeed by someone determined to be known at any cost.

The story

In 356 BC a man named Herostratus set fire to the Temple of Artemis at Ephesus, one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world. When he confessed he had done it to make his name live on, the Ephesians reportedly forbade anyone to speak or record his name. But a historian wrote it down, and so it has come down to us.

The lens

In an age full of stunts done just for attention, the ancient remedy of not naming the culprit is surprisingly current. It connects directly to today’s debate over whether news reports should withhold attackers’ names.

Drop it casually

“The more we amplify rage-bait posts, the more we play into their hands. Let’s not give them Herostratic fame.”
