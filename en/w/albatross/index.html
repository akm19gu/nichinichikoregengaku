Short-tailed albatross

Living Things

Category: Living Things生 き 物

In short

A large seabird with a wingspan of more than two meters. Its Japanese name, ahōdori, means “fool bird,” because it showed no fear of people and was easy to catch on land.

The story

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, on Torishima and other islands south of Tokyo, the birds were killed for their feathers, by some estimates in the millions. At one point the species was thought extinct, but in 1951 a handful of survivors were rediscovered on Torishima. Thanks to decades of conservation work by researchers, their numbers have slowly recovered.

The lens

Some people involved in protecting the bird have called for restoring an old name, okinotayū, roughly “noble lord of the open sea.” A cruel name given by humans to a bird humans drove to the brink, and the push to change it is itself a form of human remorse.

Drop it casually

“The short-tailed albatross was once called the noble lord of the open sea. Don’t judge it by its Japanese nickname, fool bird.”
