Parable of the raft

Religion & Myth

Category: Religion & Myth宗 教神 話

In short

The Buddha’s parable: teachings are like a raft for crossing a river, not something to carry on your back once you’re across.

The story

According to the scriptures, the Buddha said that a person who has crossed a great river on a raft would be foolish to hoist it onto his head and keep carrying it just because it had been useful. Even the teachings, he said, are to be let go once they’ve served their purpose.

The lens

We often cling to methods that once worked or rules we learned long ago. Across more than two thousand years, the parable echoes Wittgenstein’s ladder.

Drop it casually

“Maybe it’s time to treat the way I learned things when I joined like the raft in the parable and let it go.”
