Ostracism

Politics & Law

Category: Politics & Law政 治法

In short

A practice in ancient Athens in which citizens voted by scratching names on pottery shards to exile someone who might become a tyrant for ten years.

The story

In fifth-century BC Athens, citizens decided once a year whether to hold an ostracism, and if so they scratched names onto shards. According to the historian Plutarch, the statesman Aristides, known as “the Just,” was asked by an illiterate citizen to write “Aristides” on his shard. When he asked why, the man said he was sick of hearing everyone call Aristides “the Just.” Aristides quietly wrote his own name.

The lens

The English word ostracism comes from ostrakon, a pottery shard. A system meant to prevent the concentration of power could also become a tool for driving out the prominent and the popular.

Drop it casually

“Stand out too much and you risk being ostracized. Ancient Athens and today’s office aren’t so different.”
