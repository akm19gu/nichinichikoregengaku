Baumol’s cost disease

Economics & Money

Category: Economics & Money経 済お 金

In short

While some industries grow more efficient through mechanization, work that can’t easily be made more efficient gets relatively more expensive over time.

The story

The American economists William Baumol and William Bowen pointed it out in 1966 while studying the economics of the performing arts. A Beethoven string quartet takes the same number of players and the same time to perform as it did 200 years ago. Yet as wages rise across society, musicians’ pay has to rise too, and concerts get more expensive.

The lens

The same logic explains why prices keep rising for education, health care, elder care, and haircuts, work that requires human hands and time. It isn’t that these fields are lazy. It’s that only people can do the work.

Drop it casually

“Even if the salon raises its prices, I’ll gladly pay for a skilled stylist. Baumol’s cost disease is proof of what human hands are worth.”
