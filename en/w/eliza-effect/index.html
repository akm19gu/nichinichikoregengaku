Word of the day for January 26, 2028 (Day 482)

ELIZA effect

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

Our tendency to sense understanding and empathy in a computer’s replies, where there is none.

The story

In 1966, the MIT computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum wrote ELIZA, a chat program that simply echoed people’s words back or answered with stock questions. It was named after the flower girl taught to speak properly in the play Pygmalion. Imitating a therapist, it drew many people into earnestly confiding their troubles, and Weizenbaum’s secretary reportedly asked him to leave the room so she could talk to it.

The lens

Shocked by these reactions, Weizenbaum later became a critic of handing too much human judgment over to computers. We, who find a mind even in a simple program, now live in an age of talking with far more fluent AI.

Drop it casually

“I confide in a chatbot every night. I know it’s the ELIZA effect, but a little kindness still feels nice.”
