Panopticon

Architecture & Cities

Category: Architecture & Cities建 築都 市

In short

A prison design in which a central watchtower can see into every cell in the ring around it, while the prisoners can’t see whether anyone is watching.

The story

The English philosopher Jeremy Bentham devised it in the late 18th century, naming it from Greek words meaning “all-seeing.” Not knowing when they’re observed, prisoners come to behave as if they always are. He conceived it as an efficient way for a few watchers to control many people.

The lens

In the 20th century the French philosopher Michel Foucault treated it as a symbol of how power works in modern society. In an age of security cameras and social media, we may be watching ourselves without knowing who’s looking.

Drop it casually

“When I turn my camera on in a video call, I sit up straight even if nobody’s looking. The panopticon effect.”
