Berkson’s paradox

Math & Computing

Category: Math & Computing数 理情 報

In short

When you look only at a group selected by certain criteria, an apparent link can appear between two traits that are actually unrelated.

The story

The American medical statistician Joseph Berkson pointed it out in 1946. Study only hospital patients, and two diseases that have nothing to do with each other can seem linked, because the people who end up in the hospital tend to have one condition or the other.

The lens

If good-looking people seem to have worse personalities, it may be because you never spend time with people who are neither good-looking nor nice. Think about how the people in front of you were selected, and many “everybody knows” patterns start to look different.

Drop it casually

“Maybe popular restaurants only seem to have rude service because of Berkson’s paradox. I never go to places with bad food and bad service in the first place.”
