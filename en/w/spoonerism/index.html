Spoonerism

Language

Category: Language言 葉

In short

A slip of the tongue that swaps the opening sounds of two words, producing an unexpected meaning.

The story

It’s named after the Reverend William Spooner, warden of New College, Oxford, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. He’s famous for anecdotes like scolding a student who “hissed my mystery lectures” when he meant “missed my history lectures.” Many of these stories, though, are thought to have been invented later.

The lens

Slips of the tongue offer clues to how we assemble sounds in our heads before we speak. Sounds trade places because the mouth is already preparing the next word.

Drop it casually

“Nervous at the podium, I said “our queer old dean” instead of “our dear old queen.” A spoonerism moment.”
