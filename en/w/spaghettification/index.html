Spaghettification

Space

Category: Space宇 宙

In short

What happens to an object falling toward a black hole: powerful tidal forces stretch it lengthwise and squeeze it sideways until it’s long and thin, like spaghetti.

The story

Fall feet-first toward a black hole and your feet are pulled harder than your head, so your body is stretched. The British physicist Stephen Hawking popularized the word in his bestseller A Brief History of Time and elsewhere. In 2020, astronomers actually observed a star being torn apart by a black hole.

The lens

Around a supermassive black hole billions of times the sun’s mass, however, the tidal forces near the boundary are surprisingly gentle, so you’d cross it before being spaghettified. It’s strange that the bigger the monster, the gentler its doorway.

Drop it casually

“Deadlines pulling on my feet, appointments pulling on my head. I’m being spaghettified.”
